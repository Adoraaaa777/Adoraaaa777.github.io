Digital Governance and Corruption Control

01 / Business question

Business question

Business question: beyond headline GDP and market size, which institutional signals help explain whether a market will be transparent and predictable for operations? This project tests whether technology-enabled transparency improves governance outcomes only in democracies, or across institutional environments.

02 / Analysis approach

Analysis approach

V-Dem panel data, 174 countries, 2010-2022 · R 4.3.1 · two-way fixed effects with country-clustered standard errors · robustness and heterogeneity tests

What I did

I prepared and analyzed a 174-country, 2010-2022 panel dataset in R, selected indicators for corruption, information freedom, and regime type, then ran fixed-effects and robustness tests to separate the relationship from country-level differences and global yearly shocks.

Panel data

Data that follows many countries over multiple years. It helps compare both differences between markets and change within each market over time.

Two-way fixed effects

A method that controls for stable country characteristics and shared global events, making the estimated relationship more credible.

03 / Evidence

Evidence

The baseline coefficient for information freedom is -0.322 (statistically significant). The effect remains robust for executive corruption (-0.486) and public-sector corruption (-0.297). Interaction effects by regime type are not statistically significant, suggesting that transparency signals retain analytical value across regime types.

04 / GTM / operations implications

GTM / operations implications

For international business teams, digital transparency and institutional quality can supplement market-selection frameworks with operational-risk signals. The work demonstrates how cross-country data can support prioritization, risk sensing, and more grounded interpretation of local policy environments.
