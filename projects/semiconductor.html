PROJECT 01 / INDUSTRY STRATEGY

Semiconductor Industry & Policy Risk Analysis

Trade restrictions create different pressures across the semiconductor value chain, while the personal event-study extension finds more positive cumulative abnormal returns among larger firms after the policy announcement.

01 / Business question

Business question

Business question: when a policy shock changes cross-border access to chips, which firms can adapt fastest, and what operating levers explain the difference? The project has two connected layers: a collaborative multiple-case study of NVIDIA, Ambarella, and Applied Materials, followed by my individual quantitative extension on firm size and stock-market response.

02 / Analysis approach

Analysis approach

Collaborative layer: comparative case study and policy-event review. Individual extension: R-based event study of 6 U.S. semiconductor firms, with a 120-trading-day estimation period and a 7-day event window [t-3, t+3]. Firms were grouped into 3 large-cap and 3 small-cap companies; China revenue exposure was controlled within 10%-25%.

What I did

I first compared how three firms in design, fabless chips, and equipment adjusted products, customers, and production. I then independently selected a controlled six-firm sample, calculated daily returns in R, estimated normal market movement, and compared the policy-period reaction by firm size.

Event Study

A way to isolate whether a specific announcement caused an unusual stock-market reaction, after accounting for normal market movement.

CAR

Cumulative Abnormal Return. It adds up the unusual stock-price movement during the event window, showing the overall market reaction.

03 / Evidence

Evidence

NVIDIA used product adjustment and regional customer rebalancing; Ambarella faced higher exposure because it had less scale and less room to redirect supply; Applied Materials diversified markets and expanded U.S. capacity. The individual CAR analysis finds a positive association between market capitalization and CAR. Small-cap firms showed the widest dispersion, from about -5% to above +10%, so the result is presented as a preliminary finding from a small controlled sample.

04 / GTM / operations implications

GTM / operations implications

GTM and operations teams can use this framework to turn geopolitical signals into a workable exposure map: customer concentration, supplier dependency, product compliance, and regional alternatives. It supports earlier scenario planning instead of reactive market switching.
